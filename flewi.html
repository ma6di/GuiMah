Case study — Flewi

Flewi Flashcards that stick.

A live spaced-repetition platform — Spaces, Smart Add, a real SRS engine — on iOS, Android and the web. It started by fixing the hardest part of German. It now opens to every language and every subject.

01 — Why it was built

Start with one sharp pain. Ship the platform around it.

German noun gender and irregular plurals are where most learners stall — three articles, almost no reliable rules, and tools that either dump raw cards on you or gamify the grammar away. That pain was the wedge.

Flewi was built as a real product around it: a custom spaced-repetition engine, Artikel practice, auto-parsed cards, decks, streaks and native apps. The same foundations now power Spaces, Smart Add and general flashcards — so the live product is already broader than the problem it started with.

02 — Market research

Three big tools. One gap between them.

Anki

Serious spaced repetition, brutal UX, no language intelligence. You build every card by hand.

Duolingo

Great retention loops, shallow depth — weak dictionary, no real user-owned decks, thin on grammar tables.

Quizlet

Easy sharing, generic cards — no auto-parsed grammar, articles or conjugations.

The gap

Nothing combined dictionary-grade auto-parsing — article, plural, conjugation, examples, pronunciation — with a serious SRS and a native-feeling UI. Flewi sits in that middle: Anki depth without Anki friction, for motivated self-learners.

03 — Design decisions

Three calls that shaped the whole product.

One card model, three ways in

Smart Add, Manual Builder and Freestyle all land on the same idea: a card is a title plus ordered fields. That model is what lets Flewi stretch from German grammar tables to any subject.

Auto-parsed, then user-owned

Search a word and get article, plural, conjugation, example and TTS generated for you — then pick which pieces become card fields. Depth without forcing a fixed template.

Native where it matters

Ship a gesture-driven PWA first, then back it with real iOS and Android builds. Mobile is now the product source of truth; web is catching up to full parity.

04 — Architecture & stack

Self-hosted, containerised, boring on purpose.

Frontend

React (Vite) and Tailwind, with custom gesture and animation logic, shipped as a PWA — service workers and web manifest.

Mobile

Expo / React Native — the source of truth for product UX as of v2.0, including Spaces, Quest and the modular card system.

Backend

FastAPI (async Python) with SQLAlchemy and Alembic, PostgreSQL for relational data, Redis for cache, sessions and ephemeral state.

Infrastructure

Fully Dockerised services — frontend, API, database, cache, proxy — behind a Caddy reverse proxy with automatic TLS, self-hosted on Hetzner with zero-downtime deploys.

Key decision

Backend and mobile first, web parity deferred: ship the hardest surface — native — on a stable API, then port the full loop back to the web.

05 — What ships today

The German wedge opens into a learning platform.

Flewi→Flashcard Master

Spaces & Dashboard

Live on mobile. Instead of one implicit German vocabulary, every user gets self-contained Spaces — Language Spaces with lookup, Artikel tests and conjugations, or General Spaces with freeform cards for any subject.

WhyThe addressable market moves from “German learners” to anyone who uses flashcards.

Three creation paths, one card model

Smart Add, Manual Builder and Freestyle already converge on title + ordered fields. Edits happen in place on the rendered card — one model instead of three divergent form UIs.

WhyScale the product without rebuilding card creation for every new subject.

Multi-language permanent lookup store

Kaikki ingest — 196k German entries and 5.7M form-index rows — replacing fragile scrapers, with quality gating, source archiving and CC BY-SA attribution.

WhyReliability and legal cleanliness at scale; scraping does not survive going multi-language.

Web ↔ mobile parity

Next: port the modular mobile card system back to the web SPA so Spaces, Smart Add, bulk CSV, subscriptions and SRS all work on flewi.app without store funneling.

WhyThe full product should live wherever the user already is.

One visual identity

A redesign that unifies the product around the mobile look as scope broadens — one identity across languages, subjects and platforms.

WhyA platform needs one face, not a German-app leftover.

The through-line

The same title + fields card core and the same SRS engine now scale across languages, subjects and platforms — turning a focused German wedge into a general learning product without rebuilding the foundations.
